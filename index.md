## 1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?

I found image preprocessing, particularly techniques for balancing lighting and correcting inconsistencies in microscopic image data, to be the most interesting topic covered this quarter. I am interested in how these techniques can improve image quality and, consequently, our dataset. Inconsistent brightness and contrast can affect how organoid features are identified, making preprocessing important for improving the accuracy of downstream machine learning models.

## 2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.

I would like to investigate whether we can predict when organoids are likely to die based on changes in microscopic images over time. Specifically, I am interested in identifying morphological patterns or trends that may indicate an organoid is approaching death, potentially allowing its viability to be predicted before visible signs of deterioration become apparent.

## 3. What is a potential change you’d make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?

One potential change I would make to the Quarter 1 Project would be to shift from tracking individual organoids to analyzing organoids at the population level. This would allow us to investigate broader patterns across a population and better understand overall trends in organoid growth, viability, and death.

## 4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?

I would be interested in using time-series analysis to examine how organoid morphology changes over time. This could help identify temporal patterns that precede organoid death and potentially allow us to predict when an organoid is likely to die based on its previous developmental trajectory.
